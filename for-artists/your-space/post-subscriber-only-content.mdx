Any post on your Direct Line can be kept for your subscribers. Subscribers see it in full. Members without a subscription see that something is there, and can subscribe to open it. What you keep for subscribers, and how often, is always your choice.

## How it works

When you write a Direct Line post, the gem next to **\+** keeps it for subscribers. The message box turns blue and reads **Send a subscriber message...** The gem switches off again after each post, so the next one goes to everyone unless you tap it again.

The owner and admins can post for subscribers. A subscriber post goes to all your subscribers, so it can't be sent to one group at the same time.

[Step-by-step: How do I keep a post for subscribers?](https://help.kollekt.io/en/article/how-do-i-keep-a-post-for-subscribers-10cqdss/)

## What members see

Members without a subscription see the post on your Direct Line, locked, with a gem in the middle. Tapping it opens the subscribe screen, and once they subscribe, the post opens for them.

![Locked subscriber-only post as a member sees it](/for-artists/your-space/screenshots/directline-locked-for-member.png)

Subscribers, you and your team see the post in full, like any other.

![Direct Line as a subscriber, with everything open](/for-fans/inside-kollekt/screenshots/direct-line-subscribed-feed.png)

## Ideas for subscriber posts

Some things artists keep for subscribers:

- Early demos, unreleased snippets or work in progress.
- A presale link or early access to tickets.
- A voice note from the studio or the tour bus.
- A personal thank-you or shout-out to the people who subscribe.
- Anything that feels too loose for everyone, but too good to keep to yourself.

A locked post on the Direct Line also shows members what subscribing gives them. Some artists mix posts for everyone and posts for subscribers, so both feel like there's something for them.
