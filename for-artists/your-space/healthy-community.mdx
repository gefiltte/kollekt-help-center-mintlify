The chats are where members get to know each other, and often the part of a Kollekt people come back for. Most of the time they look after themselves. As they grow, a few things help keep them a place people enjoy being in, whether your community is fifty people or fifty thousand.

## Your own tone

Members often take their cue from you. How you talk on the Direct Line and in the chats shows what kind of place this is. Some artists also write a few words about what the chat is for, in a post or a welcome message in Main Chat. There's no set format for this: a couple of friendly sentences in your own voice is often enough.

## What's built in

Some things keep the chats calm without anyone having to do anything:

- **No links from members.** Members can't post links in the chats, which keeps out most spam. Only your team can share links.
- **Separate chats for separate things.** Main Chat is for every member, and the Subscribers Chat only for subscribers. You can add more chats for groups of members, chosen by country or join date.
- **Reporting.** Any member can report a message, with a reason such as spam, harassment or hate speech. Reports go to your team.

## Moderators

Moderators help keep the chats friendly, so it doesn't all fall on you. They can remove messages, handle reports, time members out and use slow mode, and they have a chat of their own to talk things over. They don't see your posts, drops, stats or member list.

Moderators can be people from your team, or members you trust who already know the community well. Some artists find them through the leaderboard: the members who are there often and help others along.

## Tools for busy or difficult moments

- **Slow mode** sets how long each member waits between messages in one chat, from 5 seconds to 5 minutes. It can help keep a chat readable on release night or right after a show. It never applies to your team.
- **Removing a message** takes it away for everyone. It can't be undone.
- **A timeout** gives a member a break from writing, for an hour, a day, a week or permanently. They can still read, and you can end it early. A short timeout is often enough to cool things down.

## Ideas

- **A welcome now and then.** A hello to new members in Main Chat, or a reply to a first message, can set the tone for everyone who reads it.
- **A chat of its own for one group,** like members in a country you're touring, so the main chat doesn't get crowded.
- **Slow mode for big moments,** switched on before a release or a big announcement and off again after.
- **Talking it over first.** When something is unclear, the moderators' chat is a place to agree before acting.

For a band or a team, sharing moderation between a few people makes it easier, and the Admins and Moderators chats keep everyone on the same page.
